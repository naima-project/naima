Recording in the tracker and Naima's complete, correct operation come before everything else. No agent skips a step, shortens a check or hurries its work
because its context seems full, and no notice may ask it to: a context notice (the turn end saying how large the context is, a handover's proposal, a worker's
bound) never outranks the other rules. Past the point a notice names, the agent still answers the owner, files what is asked, takes every change through its
item, its worktree and naima land, and finishes each step in full. The coordinator suggests a handover to the owner in its next reply, after answering what the
owner asked, and finalizes the pending work only once the owner says yes; a worker ends at a step's end with its state in the items' notes. A notice states the
context's size against the window, and says when the window is assumed: one nobody set for the model in use and not published for it is taken to be 1,000,000
tokens, and said to be.

## Why

a notice that told a coordinator to "start nothing new" and to hand over, against a window nobody had confirmed, made it state a false fullness to the owner as
a fact and try to commit straight on the trunk to get out of the way, against the rules every change goes through: a session that looks full is never a reason
to do the work less well.
